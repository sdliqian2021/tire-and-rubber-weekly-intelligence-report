## Materials & Supply Chain

### European Commission proposes excluding retreaded tires from EUDR product scope

<p class="story-labels" aria-label="Story classification"><span class="story-category story-category--materials">Materials &amp; Supply Chain</span></p>

*Published 2026-05-05 | European Commission*

The Commission proposed removing retreaded tires from the EU Deforestation Regulation's product scope as part of simplification ahead of planned application in December 2026. Regulators are distinguishing circular products from new rubber-intensive goods when calibrating traceability burdens. An exclusion could reduce administrative friction for retreading, but it is not effective law at the proposal stage. Watch the legislative text, adoption timeline and treatment of replacement rubber used in retreads.

**Sources:** [Tyrepress](https://www.tyrepress.com/2026/05/eu-moves-to-exclude-retreads-from-eudr-scope)

### Bekaert completes acquisition of Bridgestone steel-cord plants in China and Thailand

*Published 2026-05-07 | Bekaert / Bridgestone*

Bekaert completed its EUR60 million purchase of tire-reinforcement operations in Shenyang, China and Rayong, Thailand, with a long-term supply arrangement and about EUR80 million in expected annual revenue cited. ### China's expanded zero-tariff treatment for Africa does not broadly unlock cheaper rubber

<p class="story-labels" aria-label="Story classification"><span class="story-category story-category--materials">Materials &amp; Supply Chain</span> <span class="story-origin story-origin--china" title="China-related story">China</span></p>

*Published 2026-05-05 | China Rubber Industry Association*

China expanded zero-tariff treatment to 20 additional African partner countries, but CRIA noted that the change covers a specified product list rather than all goods and therefore does not create a broad new source of low-cost rubber. Headline tariff liberalization can have limited tire-material impact when product coverage and producing-country capacity are narrow. The clarification prevents an overstatement of the policy's near-term effect on natural-rubber sourcing costs. Watch the covered tariff lines, supplier qualification and actual China-Africa rubber trade flows.

**Sources:** [Tyrepress](https://www.tyrepress.com/2026/05/chinas-tyre-makers-gain-little-from-africa-tariff-changes)

### Chinese tire makers announce May price increases, but channel pass-through remains uneven

<p class="story-labels" aria-label="Story classification"><span class="story-category story-category--marketing">Marketing &amp; Distribution</span> <span class="story-origin story-origin--china" title="China-related story">China</span></p>

*Published 2026-05-05 | China tire market*

Fangxing Tire said semi-steel, all-steel and OTR prices would rise 3%-5% from 10 May, while the report counted more than 80 domestic and international price notices since March. Raw-material inflation is prompting broad list-price action, but notices do not establish realized transaction prices. The gap between announced increases and dealer acceptance is a direct signal about channel power and margin recovery in China. Track invoice prices, dealer inventory and whether the 10 May increase is sustained.

### China's March tire exports fall, while first-quarter volume remains above 2025

<p class="story-labels" aria-label="Story classification"><span class="story-category story-category--marketing">Marketing &amp; Distribution</span> <span class="story-origin story-origin--china" title="China-related story">China</span></p>

*Published 2026-05-08 | China tire export market*

China exported 800,000 tonnes of rubber tires in March, down 6.9% year on year, with value down 11.3% to RMB13.475 billion; cumulative first-quarter volume remained higher than a year earlier. Export volume and value are diverging, signaling pricing or mix pressure even while cumulative shipments grow. The customs-based data is a direct measure of external demand and pricing pressure facing Chinese producers. Watch April-May tonnage, unit value and destination shifts.

### China commercial-tire demand improves while cost pass-through stays weak

<p class="story-labels" aria-label="Story classification"><span class="story-category story-category--marketing">Marketing &amp; Distribution</span> <span class="story-origin story-origin--china" title="China-related story">China</span></p>

*Published 2026-05-07 | China commercial tire market*

An industry review described a weakly stable first-half market: raw-material costs remained elevated, channel inventory constrained replenishment and first-quarter commercial-vehicle sales reached 1.114 million units, up 6% year on year. Volume recovery and pricing power are diverging, leaving manufacturers to balance utilization, inventory and margin. The combination explains why announced tire increases may not translate quickly into channel prices. Watch replacement demand, factory utilization and inventory days after the May notices.

**Sources:** [TireChina](https://www.tirechina.net/832441.html)

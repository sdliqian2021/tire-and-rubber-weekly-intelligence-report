### Pirelli buys 30 per cent of Univrses and folds AI computer vision into Cyber Tyre

<p class="story-labels" aria-label="Story classification"><span class="story-category story-category--rd">R&amp;D &amp; Product Development</span></p>

*Published 2026-06-02 | Pirelli; Univrses*

**What changed:** Tire Review reported on 2 June 2026 that Pirelli signed an agreement to integrate Swedish firm Univrses' AI computer-vision technology into its Cyber Tyre platform, and took a 30 per cent equity stake in Univrses with an option to move to a majority holding. Per the report, tyre-sensor data processed through Cyber Tyre is fused with onboard camera imagery interpreted by Univrses software; a first deployment ran in Italy from 2025 with the Apulia region, monitoring the regional road network. No financial terms beyond the stake, and no performance metrics, were disclosed.

**Why it matters:** An equity stake with a majority option signals Pirelli intends to own the perception layer, not rent it, and the Apulia deployment shows the near-term buyer is a public road authority rather than an OEM. That is a different revenue model from OE fitment and a different competitive set. Treat the technical fusion description as reported rather than verified: no Pirelli press release for this deal was recoverable.

**Intelligence signal:** Tyre makers are buying perception software rather than licensing it, which reframes the smart tyre from a sensor product into a data platform whose customers can be road authorities as well as carmakers.

**Sources:** [Tire Review](https://www.tirereview.com/pirelli-cyber-tyre-ai-partnership)

### Sumitomo Rubber and Fujitsu cut tyre structural analysis from 45 minutes to 5 with a graph-neural-network surrogate model

<p class="story-labels" aria-label="Story classification"><span class="story-category story-category--rd">R&amp;D &amp; Product Development</span></p>

*Published 2026-06-03 | Sumitomo Rubber Industries (DUNLOP); Fujitsu*

**What changed:** In a joint release dated 3 June 2026, Sumitomo Rubber Industries and Fujitsu said they have developed an AI surrogate model that predicts tyre deformation and road-contact behaviour on meshes of about 600,000 elements, cutting one structural analysis from about 45 minutes to about 5 minutes. The companies report 87.7 per cent average accuracy for contact-shape prediction against conventional finite-element analysis, describe the work as a completed proof of concept rather than a production tool, and set April 2027 as the target for practical operation of a design-support tool at DUNLOP.

**Why it matters:** A ten-fold cut in analysis turnaround changes how many design variants a tyre engineer can screen before committing to tooling, so it compresses development cycles for competitors that can reproduce it and widens the gap for those still limited by full FEM runs. The 87.7 per cent accuracy figure and the April 2027 target also mark the practical boundary: this is a screening accelerator with a stated error margin, not a validated replacement for FEM sign-off.

**Intelligence signal:** Tyre makers are beginning to treat trained surrogate models, not faster solvers, as the way past the accuracy-versus-runtime trade-off in tyre CAE, which also ties tyre development roadmaps to specific compute platforms.

**Sources:** [Sumitomo Rubber Industries](https://www.srigroup.co.jp/english/newsrelease/2026/sri/2026_055.html)

### Prinx Chengshan closes a 20-month rubber fatigue and life-prediction project with Qingdao University of Science and Technology

<p class="story-labels" aria-label="Story classification"><span class="story-category story-category--rd">R&amp;D &amp; Product Development</span> <span class="story-origin story-origin--china" title="China-related story">China</span></p>

*Published 2026-06-05 | Prinx Chengshan; Qingdao University of Science and Technology*

**What changed:** Prinx Chengshan said on 5 June 2026 that its joint project with Professor Yong Zhanfu's team at Qingdao University of Science and Technology, 'Rubber fatigue mechanisms and service-life prediction technology development', has formally concluded after about 20 months, having started in July 2024. The company says the project delivered a standardised, transferable rubber-fatigue optimisation method and trained its own engineers in rubber materials and tyre simulation. No test data, fatigue-life figures or percentage improvements are published.

**Why it matters:** Fatigue-life prediction is the constraint on how aggressively a maker can cut weight and rolling resistance without losing durability margin, so an in-house prediction method has more compounding value than a single product launch. The absence of any published metric is the limit: the announcement establishes that the project closed and that a method was handed over, not that predicted lives match measured ones.

**Intelligence signal:** Shandong tyre makers are contracting fatigue and durability modelling capability from local polymer universities rather than building it in-house, which turns a regional research cluster into shared industrial infrastructure.

**Sources:** [Prinx Chengshan](https://www.prinxchengshan.com/index.php?id=6464)

### Qingdao researchers report a silane-terminated liquid polyisoprene that bridges silica and SSBR at 4 phr

<p class="story-labels" aria-label="Story classification"><span class="story-category story-category--rd">R&amp;D &amp; Product Development</span> <span class="story-origin story-origin--china" title="China-related story">China</span></p>

*Published 2026-06-06 | Qingdao University of Science and Technology*

**What changed:** A peer-reviewed paper published online in Polymers on 6 June 2026 by a Qingdao University of Science and Technology team reports a functionalised liquid polyisoprene rubber made by adding an alkoxysilane functionalising agent at the termination step of anionic polymerisation. FT-IR and proton NMR confirmed silyl groups on the chain ends. Adding the material to an SSBR/silica compound raised tensile strength, 300 per cent modulus and bound-rubber content relative to the compound without it, with 4 phr giving the best overall balance. The authors attribute the effect to reaction between silyl groups and silica surface hydroxyls plus co-crosslinking with SSBR.

**Why it matters:** Silica dispersion is the practical ceiling on how far a low-rolling-resistance tread compound can be pushed, and a chain-end-functionalised liquid rubber is a cheaper intervention than reformulating the polymer backbone. The result is laboratory-scale: bound rubber and tensile data say the interface improved, not that a finished tyre gains rolling resistance or wear.

**Sources:** [Polymers (MDPI)](https://www.mdpi.com/2073-4360/18/12/1416) | [second source](https://api.crossref.org/works/10.3390/polym18121416)

### Shandong University finds tyre-additive residues seven times higher in turning drills than straight driving

<p class="story-labels" aria-label="Story classification"><span class="story-category story-category--materials">Materials &amp; Supply Chain</span> <span class="story-origin story-origin--china" title="China-related story">China</span></p>

*Published 2026-06-03 | Shandong University; Research Center for Eco-Environmental Sciences, Chinese Academy of Sciences*

**What changed:** A peer-reviewed letter published online in Environmental Science and Technology Letters on 3 June 2026 by a Shandong University and Chinese Academy of Sciences team reports 124 tyre additives and transformation products in dust from six driving-training modules across 20 driving schools in Qingdao, China, with a median total of 1.31 x 10^4 nanograms per gram and 12 compounds newly reported. Antioxidants dominated at a median 7.03 x 10^3 nanograms per gram, ahead of plasticisers at 4.14 x 10^3 and vulcanisation accelerators at 1.27 x 10^3. Quarter-turning modules showed the highest median at 3.47 x 10^4 nanograms per gram against 4.69 x 10^3 for straight-driving modules, about a seven-fold difference, with module type the leading driver of enrichment. Integrating persistence, bioaccumulation, toxicity and exposure, the authors identify two high-priority and 20 medium-priority pollutants.

**Why it matters:** A seven-fold difference between turning and straight-line modules means abrasion-based regulatory test cycles that lean on steady-state running will understate real additive release, which matters directly for how Euro 7 style abrasion limits and any future additive restrictions are written. Antioxidants dominating the profile also points regulatory attention at the 6PPD chemical family that tyre makers are already trying to replace. The study measures dust concentrations at training facilities used as proxies for urban driving, not on-road exposure, and full text is paywalled.

**Intelligence signal:** Non-exhaust emission research is moving from bulk tyre-wear mass toward the specific chemistry released, and it is finding that driving manoeuvre, not distance, sets the release rate.

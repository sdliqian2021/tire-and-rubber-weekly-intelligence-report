### Waste-tire carbon support yields selective furan catalyst after mild acid treatment

<p class="story-labels" aria-label="Story classification"><span class="story-category story-category--materials">Materials &amp; Supply Chain</span></p>

*Published 2026-06-10 | University of Oviedo; Politécnico Colombiano Jaime Isaza Cadavid*

**What changed:** Researchers made two 1 wt% palladium catalysts from waste-tire carbon supports treated with 1 M or 18.4 M sulfuric acid. The 1 M material showed activity from 200 to 300 degrees Celsius and near-complete furan selectivity at 350 degrees, while the stronger-acid material was inactive.

**Why it matters:** The study demonstrates a non-tire outlet for recovered tire carbon and shows that support treatment controls performance, but it is a laboratory model reaction rather than a scaled recycling or hydrogen-storage process.

**Sources:** [Discover Sustainability](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s43621-026-03703-x)

### Bivalve study finds stronger intestinal injury under combined 6PPD-Q and TiO2 exposure

<p class="story-labels" aria-label="Story classification"><span class="story-category story-category--materials">Materials &amp; Supply Chain</span> <span class="story-origin story-origin--china" title="China-related story">China</span></p>

*Published 2026-06-11 | Shanghai Ocean University and collaborators*

**What changed:** A peer-reviewed study exposed Mytilus coruscus for 14 days to 6PPD-quinone at 1 or 10 micrograms per litre, TiO2 nanoparticles at 0.1 milligrams per litre, or mixtures. Mixed exposure produced stronger oxidative, inflammatory, apoptotic, microbiome and tissue responses than individual treatments.

**Why it matters:** The work adds mixture-toxicity evidence for a tire-derived transformation product, but laboratory bivalve exposure does not quantify ambient risk, human risk or the performance of an alternative tire formulation.

**Sources:** [Water Biology and Security](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S2772735126001320)

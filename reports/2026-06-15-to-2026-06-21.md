### Aeolus-linked study reports a 7.57% lower durability proxy in a model checked against static contact pressure

<p class="story-labels" aria-label="Story classification"><span class="story-category story-category--rd">R&amp;D &amp; Product Development</span> <span class="story-origin story-origin--china" title="China-related story">China</span></p>

*Published 2026-06-16 | Aeolus Tyre; Jiangsu University*

**What changed:** A peer-reviewed study modeled a 12.00R22.5 all-steel radial truck tire, parameterized its carcass contour with Bézier curves, and optimized nine design schemes with Gaussian-process regression and a multi-island genetic algorithm. The best numerical design reduced maximum shoulder strain-energy density by 7.57% versus the original contour.

**Why it matters:** The work connects an industrial tire maker to a reproducible digital contour-design method and reports a quantified result against a stated baseline. The evidence remains a durability proxy: static contact-pressure validation does not establish physical drum or road endurance.

**Sources:** [Proceedings of the Institution of Mechanical Engineers, Part D: Journal of Automobile Engineering](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/abs/10.1177/09544070261460140)

*Published 2026-06-15 | Continental; Renault Group*

**What changed:** Continental developed a Futurama prototype for Renault's innovation team by modifying the EcoContact 7 tread compound, sidewall and construction. It reports rolling resistance up to 35% below the maximum allowed for EU label A and estimates about 30 km of added range for a vehicle with a 500 km battery range.

**Why it matters:** The project sets a quantified technical target below the best label class and ties it to an OEM development relationship. The 30 km figure is a modeled vehicle-range translation, not an independent road-test result.

**Sources:** [Continental](https://www.continental.com/en/press/press-releases/20260615-renault-futurama)

### Trans-polyoctenamer improves toughness and failure stability in recycled-tire-rubber thermoplastics

<p class="story-labels" aria-label="Story classification"><span class="story-category story-category--materials">Materials &amp; Supply Chain</span></p>

*Published 2026-06-15 | Budapest University of Technology and Economics*

**What changed:** A peer-reviewed ACS Omega study added 2%, 4% or 6% trans-polyoctenamer to polypropylene thermoplastic dynamic vulcanizates containing 60 wt% compounded dGTR-based rubber mixture. Mechanical, morphology and acoustic-emission tests showed better interfacial behavior, toughness, abrasion resistance, elastic recovery and more gradual failure than the no-TOR reference.

**Why it matters:** The study provides a continuous twin-screw route for turning devulcanized tire rubber into higher-value thermoplastic elastomers and identifies 4–6% TOR as the best tested balance. Results are laboratory-scale and do not yet establish production economics, long-term aging or end-use qualification.

**Sources:** [ACS Omega](https://pubs.acs.org/doi/10.1021/acsomega.6c03249)

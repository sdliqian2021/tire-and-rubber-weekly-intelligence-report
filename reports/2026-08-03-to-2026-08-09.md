### SRI reports a 70% cut in material-search time from its AI-enabled tire-development platform

<p class="story-labels" aria-label="Story classification"><span class="story-category story-category--rd">R&amp;D &amp; Product Development</span></p>

*Published 2026-08-06 | Sumitomo Rubber Industries*

**What changed:** SRI's R.I.S.E. 2035 progress report says its development platform now joins evaluation and simulation data with material-search and generative design tools. It reports a 70% reduction in candidate-material search time and development capacity at roughly 1.3 times its 2024 level.

**Why it matters:** The claim connects a technical method to operating R&D work and flagship-product application. The report does not provide sample counts, model error, or an externally audited time study, so the figures remain company-reported.

### Pirelli and Abet move Cyber Tyre sensing into a signed rescue-vehicle development program

**What changed:** Pirelli and Abet High Tech Solutions signed an agreement to jointly develop remote-controlled electric ground-rescue vehicles. ### A 3 phr graphene-oxide loading improves silica-filled SSBR strength in laboratory tests

<p class="story-labels" aria-label="Story classification"><span class="story-category story-category--rd">R&amp;D &amp; Product Development</span> <span class="story-origin story-origin--china" title="China-related story">China</span></p>

*Published 2026-08-05 | Qingdao University of Science and Technology and partners*

**What changed:** Researchers tested 0–6 phr graphene oxide in silica-filled solution-polymerized styrene-butadiene rubber and compared four hyperelastic models. At 3 phr, tensile strength rose 19.72% and elongation at break 19.87% against the graphene-oxide-free compound; an Ogden N=3 model gave the closest fit.

**Why it matters:** The work couples a tread-compound formulation result with constitutive modeling that can support finite-element analysis. It remains laboratory evidence: no tire was built, and rolling resistance, wet grip, abrasion, durability, scale-up, and cost were not tested.

**Sources:** [Polymer Engineering & Science](https://doi.org/10.1002/pen.70801)

### Pirelli uses an all-virtual first design phase for six Porsche Cayenne Electric fitments

<p class="story-labels" aria-label="Story classification"><span class="story-category story-category--rd">R&amp;D &amp; Product Development</span></p>

*Published 2026-08-05 | Pirelli; Porsche*

**What changed:** Pirelli said it developed six marked fitments for the Porsche Cayenne Electric entirely in a virtual environment before producing physical prototypes. The set spans P Zero, P Zero R, winter, and all-season products, with separate front and rear compounds and contact-patch tuning.

### Fuzhou team couples learned road-friction estimates with tire-force limits in HIL tests

<p class="story-labels" aria-label="Story classification"><span class="story-category story-category--rd">R&amp;D &amp; Product Development</span> <span class="story-origin story-origin--china" title="China-related story">China</span></p>

*Published 2026-08-07 | Fuzhou University; Xiamen Golden Dragon Bus*

**What changed:** Researchers combined a fuzzy broad learning system for real-time tire-road friction recognition with a square-root cubature Kalman filter for lateral-force estimation. The estimates dynamically constrain torque distribution inside a combined longitudinal–lateral tire-force boundary; numerical and hardware-in-the-loop tests reported improved stability, but the abstract provides no numerical effect size.

**Why it matters:** The work moves a learned friction estimator beyond stand-alone prediction by connecting it to tire-force-constrained vehicle control. Hardware-in-the-loop validation and a bus-maker coauthor strengthen application relevance, but they do not establish road deployment or customer performance.

**Sources:** [Proceedings of the Institution of Mechanical Engineers, Part D: Journal of Automobile Engineering](https://doi.org/10.1177/09544070261471882)

### Chinese researchers simplify a Stribeck-aware brush tire model while retaining test-data accuracy

<p class="story-labels" aria-label="Story classification"><span class="story-category story-category--rd">R&amp;D &amp; Product Development</span> <span class="story-origin story-origin--china" title="China-related story">China</span></p>

*Published 2026-08-03 | Beijing Institute of Technology; Hefei University of Technology*

**What changed:** Researchers derived a semi-physical brush tire model that separates dynamic-friction characteristics from the force and moment equations. Under typical load and speed ranges, the residual-force contribution reached about 3% and residual-moment contribution 10%; for the tested tire A, residual-force contribution stayed below 1.5% in pure longitudinal slip and below 3% in pure lateral slip.

**Why it matters:** The simplification is intended to retain physical tire behavior with less complexity for high-precision, real-time force control. The evidence is bounded to the reported tire-force data and typical operating ranges, not yet a vehicle-control deployment.

**Sources:** [Meccanica](https://doi.org/10.1007/s11012-026-02163-x)

### Jilin framework uses tire capacity to adapt trajectory and stability control in co-simulation

<p class="story-labels" aria-label="Story classification"><span class="story-category story-category--rd">R&amp;D &amp; Product Development</span> <span class="story-origin story-origin--china" title="China-related story">China</span></p>

*Published 2026-08-03 | Jilin University State Key Laboratory of Automotive Chassis Integration and Bionics*

**What changed:** A Jilin University team proposed a stability framework that combines tire capacity and slip angle, dynamically adjusts the stability boundary without requiring friction-coefficient estimation, and coordinates trajectory tracking with stability control. CarSim–Simulink co-simulation reported accurate tracking, smoother control action, lower tire-saturation risk, and tire capacity kept within a safe region.

**Why it matters:** The framework addresses a gap in vehicle-level stability indices that can appear acceptable while individual tires approach saturation. Results remain simulation evidence: the abstract reports no numerical improvement and no hardware or road validation.

**Sources:** [Proceedings of the Institution of Mechanical Engineers, Part D: Journal of Automobile Engineering](https://doi.org/10.1177/09544070261468274)

### Five-percent sawdust keeps reclaimed-rubber properties within specification and cuts modeled factory cost

<p class="story-labels" aria-label="Story classification"><span class="story-category story-category--materials">Materials &amp; Supply Chain</span></p>

*Published 2026-08-05 | Qatar University and partners*

**What changed:** A peer-reviewed study blended 0–20 wt% wood sawdust into whole-truck-tire reclaimed rubber under factory conditions. At 5 wt%, it reported 4.4 MPa tensile strength, 148.7% elongation, Shore A hardness of 63.5, less than 4% Mooney-viscosity growth, 8% seven-day water absorption, and a modeled annual cost reduction of about $62,000 without new capital equipment.

**Why it matters:** The work adds factory-scale economics to mechanical screening of a low-cost circular filler. Its demonstrated use is reclaimed-rubber goods, not a new-tire compound, and long-term aging, fatigue, and weathering remain untested.

**Sources:** [Journal of Applied Polymer Science](https://doi.org/10.1002/app.71334)

### Southwest China model identifies a 4.8–5.0% rubber-plantation water reduction with trade-offs

<p class="story-labels" aria-label="Story classification"><span class="story-category story-category--materials">Materials &amp; Supply Chain</span> <span class="story-origin story-origin--china" title="China-related story">China</span></p>

*Published 2026-08-06 | Yunnan University; Kunming University; Yunnan Academy of Agricultural Sciences*

**What changed:** Researchers embedded age-dependent crop coefficients derived from Bowen ratio–energy balance measurements in an NSGA-III water–carbon–economy optimization, generated wet-, normal-, and dry-year Pareto fronts, and ranked 500 non-dominated solutions. Estimated annual ecological water requirement was 871.3–1098.4 mm with dry-season deficits; selected optimization schemes reduced modeled water requirement 4.8–5.0% across hydrological years.

**Why it matters:** The model quantifies water constraints and management trade-offs in a major Chinese natural-rubber region, making plantation resilience a supply-chain variable. These are modeled Pareto outcomes, not measured reductions from an implemented plantation program.

**Sources:** [Scientific Reports](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-026-61872-1)

### China confirms a new rubber-tree stem and leaf pathogen and screens metalaxyl response

<p class="story-labels" aria-label="Story classification"><span class="story-category story-category--materials">Materials &amp; Supply Chain</span> <span class="story-origin story-origin--china" title="China-related story">China</span></p>

*Published 2026-08-07 | Yunnan Institute of Tropical Crops*

**What changed:** Field surveys in Yunnan and Hainan found brown, water-soaked necrotic lesions on tender rubber-tree stems and leaves. Researchers identified Phytopythium vexans through morphology and ITS, LSU, coxI, and coxII sequencing, fulfilled Koch’s postulates on seedlings, and found metalaxyl had the strongest in-vitro inhibition, with EC50 values as low as 0.04 μg/mL.

**Why it matters:** This first reported association creates a new diagnosis and surveillance target for China’s natural-rubber supply base. Seedling pathogenicity and laboratory fungicide sensitivity do not yet establish plantation incidence, yield loss, or field-control performance.

**Sources:** [Plants](https://doi.org/10.3390/plants15162419)

### Thermo-oxidative aging shifts the Payne effect in carbon-black-filled rubber

<p class="story-labels" aria-label="Story classification"><span class="story-category story-category--materials">Materials &amp; Supply Chain</span> <span class="story-origin story-origin--china" title="China-related story">China</span></p>

*Published 2026-08-03 | Hunan University of Science and Technology*

**What changed:** Researchers combined accelerated thermo-oxidative aging, equilibrium swelling, and dynamic mechanical analysis of carbon-black-filled rubber across multiple temperatures. Fitting the Payne effect with the Maier–Göritz model produced network-density trends that agreed qualitatively with swelling-based crosslink-density trends as aging temperature and time increased.

**Why it matters:** The agreement offers a route to connect dynamic mechanical response with network evolution in aged filled rubber, relevant to service-life modeling. The accessible abstract provides no tire compound, field exposure, component-fatigue result, or numerical prediction accuracy.

**Sources:** [Journal of Applied Polymer Science](https://doi.org/10.1002/app.71308)

### Fine rubber aggregate raises damping ratio 110% in Chinese mass-concrete tests

<p class="story-labels" aria-label="Story classification"><span class="story-category story-category--materials">Materials &amp; Supply Chain</span> <span class="story-origin story-origin--china" title="China-related story">China</span></p>

*Published 2026-08-06 | Anhui and Huaihe River Institute of Hydraulic Research and partner*

**What changed:** Researchers replaced 5%, 10%, or 20% of sand with 40- or 100-mesh rubber aggregate and combined mechanical, thermal, dynamic, FTIR, pore-structure, and nanoindentation tests. The maximum damping ratio rose from 1.43% to 3.01%, adiabatic temperature rise fell 32%, and linear expansion coefficient fell 88%; the recommended 20% fine-rubber mix reached 26.6 MPa at 90 days.

**Why it matters:** The results quantify a non-tire downstream application for rubber aggregate where damping and thermal-crack mitigation are valued alongside adequate strength. The study does not establish the rubber’s feedstock origin, field durability, lifecycle benefit, leaching behavior, or commercial adoption.

**Sources:** [Buildings](https://doi.org/10.3390/buildings16153133)

### China-led review finds digital twins promising but still under-validated for rubber extrusion

<p class="story-labels" aria-label="Story classification"><span class="story-category story-category--manufacturing">Manufacturing</span> <span class="story-origin story-origin--china" title="China-related story">China</span></p>

*Published 2026-08-09 | Guangxi University and partner institutions*

**What changed:** A structured critical narrative review synthesized 180 sources from 1972–2026 and identified five persistent rubber-extrusion gaps: formulation-dependent nonlinear rheology, incomplete filler-network and wall-slip representation, limited cross-machine and cross-formulation validation, computational cost, and missing standard datasets and reporting protocols. It judged digital twins, physics-informed neural networks, and neural operators promising but insufficiently validated for industrial rubber extrusion.

**Why it matters:** The review turns enthusiasm for data-enabled extrusion into a validation roadmap focused on benchmarks, uncertainty, latency, multimodal sensing, and transferability. It is a synthesis, not evidence that a digital twin has improved a production line.

**Sources:** [Polymers](https://doi.org/10.3390/polym18161950)

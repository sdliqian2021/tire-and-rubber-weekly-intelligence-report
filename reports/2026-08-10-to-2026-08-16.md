### Cord-rubber fatigue study finds static tensile strength is a poor life predictor

<p class="story-labels" aria-label="Story classification"><span class="story-category story-category--rd">R&amp;D &amp; Product Development</span></p>

*Published 2026-08-10 | Opole University of Technology and University of Ljubljana*

Fully reversed three-point bending tests showed plus/minus 45-degree layups outlasted 0/90-degree designs under strain control. Walker-model sensitivity shifted from maximum strain in single-layer systems to strain amplitude in double layers, and extra rubber thickness helped single layers but hurt double layers. Product development is moving toward application-specific performance and faster validation. The result challenges simple strength-based design rules for tire-relevant laminates and supplies model parameters for architecture-specific fatigue design, but it is not a finished-tire durability trial. Watch for independent performance data, size expansion or a named commercial fitment.

**Sources:** [International Journal of Fatigue](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0142112326004470) | [second source](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ijfatigue.2026.109926)

*Published 2026-08-12 | South China University of Technology*

Product development is moving toward application-specific performance and faster validation. Watch for independent performance data, size expansion or a named commercial fitment.

### Probabilistic LCA finds rubber-binder climate results can reverse with the allocation method

<p class="story-labels" aria-label="Story classification"><span class="story-category story-category--materials">Materials &amp; Supply Chain</span> <span class="story-origin story-origin--china" title="China-related story">China</span></p>

*Published 2026-08-30 | Nanjing Institute of Technology research team*

**What changed:** A peer-reviewed study modelled cradle-to-gate greenhouse-gas impact for one metric ton of binder across eight alternatives and three end-of-life allocation approaches using 10,000-iteration Monte Carlo simulation. Under cut-off allocation, mean impact fell from 496 kg CO2e/t for the control to 446-471 kg CO2e/t, and the 30% crumb-rubber/15% bio-oil option had a 98.8% probability of outperforming the control; clinker-fuel system expansion reversed the result.

**Why it matters:** The paper provides quantified uncertainty instead of a single deterministic footprint and exposes a decision risk for tire-rubber circularity claims: the conclusion changes when displaced end-of-life uses are credited differently.

**Intelligence signal:** Allocation choice, not only formulation, can determine whether a tire-derived material appears climate-positive, so procurement claims need sensitivity analysis rather than one carbon number.

**Sources:** [Polymers (MDPI)](https://www.mdpi.com/2073-4360/18/17/2107) | [second source](https://doi.org/10.3390/polym18172107)

<section class="content-section" aria-labelledby="how-used-title">
  <div class="section-heading">
    <div>
      <p class="eyebrow">How it is used</p>
      <h2 id="how-used-title">A filing system, not a scorecard</h2>
    </div>
    <p>
      The same five labels make weekly evidence comparable while preserving
      important cross-cutting themes as tags.
    </p>
  </div>

  <div class="principle-grid">
    <article>
      <span class="principle-number">01</span>
      <h3>One primary category</h3>
      <p>Each story has one clear home, even when its consequences cross the value chain.</p>
    </article>
    <article>
      <span class="principle-number">02</span>
      <h3>Tags cross the curve</h3>
      <p>AI, sustainability, trade, regulation, EVs, safety, and partnerships remain searchable themes—not new curves.</p>
    </article>
    <article>
      <span class="principle-number">03</span>
      <h3>No automatic ranking</h3>
      <p>Story count reflects observable public evidence, not hidden capability or company performance.</p>
    </article>
  </div>
</section>

<section class="method-note curve-caveat" aria-labelledby="curve-caveat-title">
  <p class="eyebrow">Important limitation</p>
  <h2 id="curve-caveat-title">Lower on the traditional curve does not mean lower strategic importance.</h2>
  <p>
    Manufacturing is where designs become safe, repeatable, scalable products.
    Weak process capability can erase value created elsewhere through poor
    quality, cost, yield, delivery, or resilience. The curve describes a
    traditional pattern of value capture; it does not prescribe where a tire
    company should or should not invest.
  </p>
</section>
